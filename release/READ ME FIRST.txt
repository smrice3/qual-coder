QUAL CODER {VERSION}

A free tool for coding qualitative data: interview transcripts, field notes,
open-ended survey answers. It runs on your own computer and opens in your
web browser. Your projects stay on your computer.
[[mac,windows]]

Everything Qual Coder needs is in this folder, so there's nothing else to
install.
[[end]]


1. START QUAL CODER

[[windows]]
  a. Unzip the download first: right-click the zip file, choose
     "Extract All", then click Extract. (Double-clicking the zip only shows
     a preview, and Qual Coder can't start from there.)

  b. In the folder that opens, double-click "Start Qual Coder".

  c. The first time, Windows may say "Windows protected your PC". Click
     "More info", then "Run anyway".
[[mac]]
  a. Double-click the zip file you downloaded, unless your Mac already
     unzipped it. A "Qual Coder" folder appears next to it. You can move
     that folder anywhere you like.

  b. In that folder, double-click "Start Qual Coder.command".

  c. The first time, your Mac says it can't open the file because Apple
     can't check it for malicious software. Click Done. Then:
       - Open System Settings > Privacy & Security.
       - Scroll down and click "Open Anyway" next to
         "Start Qual Coder.command".
       - When your Mac asks again, click "Open Anyway" and enter your Mac
         password.
     If your Mac asks whether Terminal may access files in your Downloads
     folder (or wherever the Qual Coder folder is), click Allow.
     You only do all this once.
[[plain]]
  First, install Python 3.8 or newer (free), unless you already have it:

    Windows: https://www.python.org/downloads/windows/
             On the first installer screen, tick "Add python.exe to PATH".
    Mac:     https://www.python.org/downloads/macos/

  Then start Qual Coder:

    Windows: unzip the download (right-click > Extract All), then
             double-click "Start Qual Coder.bat". If Windows says "Windows
             protected your PC", click "More info", then "Run anyway".

    Mac:     double-click "Start Qual Coder.command". If macOS says it
             can't check the file for malicious software, open System
             Settings > Privacy & Security, scroll down, and click "Open
             Anyway" next to "Start Qual Coder.command".

  If double-clicking doesn't work, open Terminal (Mac) or Command Prompt
  (Windows), go to this folder, and run:
      Mac:      python3 server.py --open
      Windows:  py server.py --open
[[end]]

A window full of text opens, and then Qual Coder opens in your web browser
at http://localhost:4350. Leave that window open while you work. Closing it
stops Qual Coder. Next time, just double-click "Start Qual Coder" again.
[[mac,windows]]

If Qual Coder doesn't open, take a screenshot of that window and send it to
whoever gave you Qual Coder. The window says what went wrong.
[[end]]


2. WHERE YOUR WORK IS SAVED

In a folder called "Qual Coder Projects" in your home folder:
[[windows,plain]]
    Windows:  C:\Users\<your name>\Qual Coder Projects
[[mac,plain]]
    Mac:      /Users/<your name>/Qual Coder Projects
[[end]]

Projects save automatically, keep a backup copy every 10 minutes, and can
be exported as a single file (Export > Full project file). That file is how
you back a project up or share it with someone else, who imports it from
the Qual Coder home screen.

Keep research data only where your IRB protocol allows it to be stored.


3. GETTING A NEWER VERSION

When a new version comes out, an "Update available" button appears at the
top of Qual Coder. Click it, then click "Update now". Qual Coder updates
itself and restarts in a few seconds, and your projects aren't touched.
To check yourself, open any project and go to Settings > Updates.

Once in a while, a new version needs a fresh download instead. Qual Coder
says so and links to the download page. Download it, unzip it, and use it
instead of this folder. Your "Qual Coder Projects" folder is separate, so
your work carries over.
[[mac]]
You'll do the "Open Anyway" step once more for a fresh download.
[[end]]

To find new versions, Qual Coder looks at its page on GitHub. It never
sends anything about your projects.


4. FILES YOU CAN BRING IN

Word (.docx), text (.txt, .md), Zoom or Teams captions (.vtt, .srt), RTF,
a spreadsheet saved as .csv (one row per document, such as survey
answers), a .zip of any of these, or pasted text. For a PDF, save it as a
Word file first or copy and paste its text.

Google Docs, two ways:
  - Google sign-in (ASU Google accounts): + Add > Google Drive > Choose
    from Google Drive. The first time, sign in with your ASU account and
    click Allow. Then pick documents in Google's own window. Qual Coder can
    open only the documents you pick, and nothing else in your Drive.
  - Drive download (works with any account): in Google Drive, select the
    documents or a whole folder, right-click > Download. Drive saves a .zip
    with each Google Doc as a Word file. In Qual Coder, choose + Add > Files
    and pick that .zip; every document in it is added.


5. OPTIONAL: AI SUGGESTIONS (LOCAL ONLY, OFF BY DEFAULT)

Qual Coder can ask an AI model running on your own computer to suggest
codes. Nothing is sent over the internet. You accept or reject every
suggestion, and accepted ones are labeled AI in all exports.

To use it:
  a. Install Ollama from https://ollama.com/download and open it.
[[mac]]
  b. Open Terminal and run:
[[windows]]
  b. Open Command Prompt and run:
[[plain]]
  b. In Terminal (Mac) or Command Prompt (Windows), run:
[[end]]
         ollama pull llama3.1:8b
     (a 5 GB download; works best with 16 GB of memory)
  c. In a project, open Settings > AI suggestions > Turn on.


6. QUICK START

  - Create a project, then use "+ Add" to bring in documents.
  - Select text, type a code name, and press Enter.
  - Press / to search every document; you can code search results in bulk.
  - Codebook: definitions and criteria. Memos: your analytic notes.
  - Analyze: read every excerpt for a code. Themes: group codes.
[[mac]]
  - Undo with Command+Z.
[[windows]]
  - Undo with Ctrl+Z.
[[plain]]
  - Undo with Ctrl+Z (Command+Z on a Mac).
[[end]]
